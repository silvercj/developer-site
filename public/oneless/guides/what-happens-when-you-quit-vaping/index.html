What happens when you quit vaping

A timeline of the first hours, days and months. Each step says how solid the evidence is.

How to read this

Two kinds of evidence sit behind a quit-vaping timeline. The first is nicotine pharmacology: how fast the body clears nicotine is measured and well understood, and it is the same whether the nicotine came from a cigarette or a vape. The second is smoking cessation research: decades of it, on cravings, withdrawal and relapse, most of it done on smokers. Vaping research is younger and thinner. So the early steps below are firm, and the later ones are what people commonly report, applied from smoking to vaping with that caveat stated.

The first day

  • 20 minutes. Your heart rate starts to settle back towards its resting level. Smoking research, applied to vaping.
  • 2 hours. The nicotine in your blood has roughly halved. Its half-life is about two hours. Pharmacology, firm.
  • 24 hours. One full day. Cravings come in waves that usually pass within a few minutes, and the first day is mostly outlasting waves. Smoking research, applied to vaping.

The first week

  • 3 days. Nicotine is gone from your blood, and its main by-product, cotinine, is nearly gone. Withdrawal is often strongest around now, then eases. Pharmacology for the clearance; reports for the peak.
  • 1 week. The first week is the hardest stretch for most people. Cravings tend to get shorter from here. Smoking research, applied to vaping.

What withdrawal feels like varies. Irritability, restlessness, poor sleep, trouble concentrating and a stronger appetite are the common ones. They are signs the body is adjusting, not signs that something is wrong, and they fade over one to a few weeks for most people.

The first months

  • 2 weeks. Sleep, mood and concentration usually settle as your body adjusts to life without nicotine.
  • 1 month. Cravings are now more habit than chemistry: the after-food one, the first-break one, the drive-home one. Each one you get through makes the next easier.
  • 3 months. Many people find cravings rare by now, and short when they do come.
  • 6 months. Six months of clearer air. Research on how lungs recover after vaping specifically is still emerging, so no firm claim is made here.
  • 1 year. A year free. The longer you stay stopped, the less likely a return becomes.

What a slip does to the timeline

Less than you fear. One vape puts a small amount of nicotine back and restarts the current stretch. It does not undo a month of adjustment, and it does not cancel the days you have already had. The useful response is to log it, work out which moment it belonged to, and plan for that moment. Treating a slip as a full relapse is the thing most likely to turn it into one.
