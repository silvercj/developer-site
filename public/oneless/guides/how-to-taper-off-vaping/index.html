How to taper off vaping: the gap method

Count the time between vapes, not the puffs. Make the gap a little longer each week until a stop date you chose.

Why gaps, not puffs

Puff counting fails for a plain reason: nobody can count puffs honestly for a month. A gap is different. You know when you last picked the vape up, and you know what time it is now. That is the whole measurement. Waiting is also the skill you need after you stop, because a craving is a wait too. Practising it before the stop date is the point of a taper.

The numbers, for a 20-a-day routine

Start from your routine. Twenty pick-ups over sixteen waking hours is a vape every 48 minutes. That is your baseline gap. A four-week taper then asks for:

  • Week 1: wait 1 hour 5 minutes between vapes, about 15 a day.
  • Week 2: 1 hour 35 minutes, about 10 a day.
  • Week 3: 2 hours 10 minutes, about 7 a day.
  • Week 4: 3 hours, about 5 a day. Then stop.

The first week is a small win on purpose. The steps get bigger as the habit loosens. Two weeks is the same curve compressed; eight weeks stretches it. Whatever the length, the gap never goes above eight hours, because a longer wait is a night's sleep, not a target you can act on.

What to do in the wait

The gap is not willpower for an hour. It is one decision at the moment the urge arrives, then a few minutes. Three things reliably fill those minutes: 45 seconds of slow breathing, a two-minute distraction with your hands, or reading the reason you wrote down when you started. Cravings come in waves and most pass within a few minutes, which is short enough to outlast on purpose.

Then log the pick-up when it comes. An honest log matters more than a good one. In OneLess a taper day only counts towards your savings once you have confirmed that its log was complete, and a day with nothing recorded counts nothing. That sounds strict. It is what keeps the numbers yours.

Slips

A vape before the gap is up is a slip, and a slip is one moment. Log it and start the next gap from there. The days you already banked, the milestones you earned and the money you kept all stay. What resets is the current wait, and that was always going to reset at the next pick-up anyway.

When a taper is not worth it

If you already pick up two times a day or fewer, you are waiting eight hours or more between vapes. There is no gap left to grow, and a taper would print your own routine back at you as if it were a plan. Set a date instead, get ready for it, and stop. The app says this plainly when your numbers fit, rather than selling you a schedule that adds nothing.

A taper also does not suit everyone who could use one. Some people find that a smaller amount of nicotine spread through the day keeps the habit alive longer than a clean break would. If you have tried tapering before and it dragged on, a stop date with preparation may be the better fit. Both routes are in the app and both are free.

Sources and limits

The gap curve is arithmetic on your own routine, not a nicotine reduction prescription, and the app never changes it without you confirming the change. Guidance on cravings and preparing to stop is drawn from the NHS quit vaping pages and the CDC's guidance on quitting e-cigarettes. If cravings feel unmanageable, talk to a pharmacist or your local stop smoking service.
